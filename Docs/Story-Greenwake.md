# Greenwake — Chapter 1 story bible (locked 2 Nov 2026)

> **Status: CANON for the Greenwake chapter.** This page owns the chapter’s story: the place, the
> bloom, the Slime King, the Guard’s choice and the chapter’s ending.
> It does **not** own exact dialogue or beat timings; those live in `Story-Nodes.md`.
> It does **not** own scene playback; that lives in `Story-Scenes.md`.
> No dialogue is written here on purpose. The story comes first.

---

## 1. The chapter in one line

The Guard enter a forest that is growing years in hours, cut out the shard feeding it, and leave with
the first piece of the Second Dawn — and the knowledge that they ended a living place to take it.

---

## 2. What the shard is

The Second Dawn stored time. When it broke, most of that time went into the rewind, but the shards
still hold concentrated time under pressure.

A shard is a **cracked vessel of stored time**, not a soul, a memory or a heart. It leaks time.

Time leaking into the world does three things:

1. **It accelerates existing life.** Plants grow in minutes. Fruit ripens and rots in seconds. Roots
   push through each other. Sap moves.
2. **It churns dead matter.** Mud, water and leaf litter move in loops. This is what the slimes are.
3. **It distorts the senses.** The air is too warm. The light looks like early morning. Sound is
   muffled. Animals avoid the pocket or die in it.

It does **not** create life. Every living plant in the bloom was already alive. The shard only forces
that life to burn too fast. The slimes are not life at all; they are matter caught in the churn.

---

## 3. The bloom

The shard’s leak is strongest at its landing point and fades with distance. It does not cover the
whole of Greenwake. It creates a **local bloom**: a pocket of forest growing years in hours.

The bloom is a real living thicket:

- real plants, real roots, real flowers and fruit;
- real animals at the edge, driven out or killed by the pressure;
- a real ecological cost when the shard is removed.

The bloom is also unsustainable. Its plants grow past what the forest can support; roots clog the
water; fruit rots before it can seed; animals cannot live there. It is a fever, not a paradise.

When the shard is removed, the leak stops. The bloom collapses under its own overgrowth. The ordinary
forest outside the pocket survives. The hollow at the center is left dead.

This is the cost of the chapter: the Guard take the pump, and the bloom dies.

---

## 4. Why Greenwake

The shard fell into the hollow of Greenwake’s oldest tree. It did not choose the place. It landed in
the deepest hollow the forest had, and the old root network carried the leak through the soil.

The tree is half dead. Its trunk is hollow, its roots are enormous, and its crown is gone. The shard
lodged inside it. Roots grew around the shard and sealed it in. The bloom spread through the root
network from there.

The player does not need the tree explained. The image is enough: an ancient hollow stump with roots
that are still moving.

---

## 5. The slimes

The slimes are not creatures. They are clumps of wet mud, leaf litter and root fibre caught in the
time churn.

They do not eat, hunt or hate. They drift toward the strongest part of the leak because the churn is
strongest there. When cut apart, they collapse into mud and reform as the leak pushes them back
together.

The Guard meet them because the road to the center is narrow and the bloom pushes them across it. The
slimes are not an army. They are the bloom’s current.

The Soldier’s line asks, “Is that a ... Slime!?” That is first contact, not a claim about what it is. The
truth is in the next paragraph: the slimes are matter moved by the leak, not creatures. Later scenes
must not turn the question into a fact.

The first slime is the single one the Guard meet at the edge. It is not a pack; it is one piece of the
bloom’s motion. It passes them, moving toward the center. The Guard follows it because it is the only
lead they have.

---

## 6. The Slime King

The Slime King is the **knot** at the center of the bloom.

At the old tree, the leak is strongest. Roots, mud, water and dead wood accumulate around the shard
and grow together. The mass becomes huge. A ring of roots grows around the top like a crown. That is
why it looks like a king.

It does not rule the slimes. It does not want the shard. It has no plan. It is the largest piece of
the bloom, wrapped around the source.

Its behaviour is protective:

- it does not attack until the Guard step between it and the shard;
- when cut, it pulls itself back around the shard instead of chasing the Guard;
- when it finally collapses, it reaches for the shard, not for the party.

The Guard read that as a guardian. The player sees a defence. The truth is simpler: the bloom is
trying to keep its power source.

When the King comes apart, it does not bleed. It collapses into mud, roots and water. The shard is
left in the hollow, warm and quiet.

---

## 7. The Guard and the choice

The Guard come because the king sent them. The order is simple: reach the shard, take it, leave before
anyone else arrives. They do not know what removing it will do to the wood.

At the hollow, the Mage understands. The bloom is alive. The shard is feeding it. Taking the shard
will end it.

The Knight makes the call. The mission is bigger than the bloom. The Soldier follows because the
Knight leads. The Mage follows because the shard cannot be left for the Black Knight. No one is
comfortable with it.

This is not a player choice. It is a character choice. The chapter’s moral weight comes from watching
the Knight choose the mission over a living place — and from the player understanding what that
choice costs before the characters say it aloud.

The Black Knight does not appear. His presence is a fact of the road, not a scene: smoke on a distant
ridge, black banners moving, birds fleeing in the wrong direction. The Guard are racing a war they
cannot see.

## 8. Space and wave progression

The stage is one place, but the player moves through it. The wave structure is also the chapter’s
geography.

| Stage waves | Place | What lives here | Story function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | The edge of Greenwake | Bats, flushed from the canopy by a root rupture | The Guard is lost; the bats panic and fly out through the party. The fight is a collision, not a hunt. |
| 3 | The edge | Bats and the first werewolf | The first predator follows the flock. The mixed wave is the transition into the wolf act. |
| 4 | The broken ground | Werewolves | The pack. A clean fight with a real threat. |
| 5 | The root lip | Slimes and the last werewolf | The last predator is caught at the wall. `S03_Wall` starts here. The Guard must cut through. |
| 6 | The root lip | Slimes | The wall is pure bloom now. Nothing else remains. |
| 7 | The heart | The old hollow tree, the Slime King, the shard | `S04_King` and the boss fight. |
| After clear | The hollow | Collapsed bloom, ordinary forest beyond | `S05_Outro`: the theft and the cost. |
| On defeat | Last ground held | The same road and enemies, wounds closed | `S06_Defeat`: the rewind. |

This progression explains the stage’s enemy pool. The bats and wolves are real animals driven out by
the bloom. The slimes are the bloom’s matter. The Slime King is the knot at the source.

The first slime is an intro actor, not a wave. It passes the Guard and they follow it. Stage 1 has six
normal waves; wave 7 is the boss.

It also makes the mid scene a real threshold instead of a random pause.

## 10. What the chapter must show

These are the concrete images the player should remember:

- no bird song and no insects, but the undergrowth is moving;
- sap running in cold air;
- fruit ripening and rotting on the branch;
- roots pushing through the road;
- water running toward the center of the wood;
- warm, wet air and a sealed-greenhouse smell;
- slimes rolling inward, not toward the party;
- the old hollow tree with a root crown;
- the Slime King covering the shard;
- the bloom going still when the shard comes out;
- the ordinary forest outside the hollow, unchanged;
- one bird returning beyond the dead pocket;
- smoke or black banners on the road ahead.

The last image matters: the forest is not dead. The bloom is. The Guard walk out through ordinary
trees, carrying the thing that made the pocket live.

---

## 11. What the first wipe means

The Guard were at the king’s door when the Second Dawn broke. The light passed through them. They are
marked.

The shard recognises that mark. When the party falls, it pulls them back to the last ground they held:
same road, same enemies, wounds closed, gear kept. It does not rewind the world. It does not undo the
bloom. It only refuses to let the marked people go.

This is not mercy. It is the morning refusing to release what it touched.

The player does not need to wipe to finish the chapter. The rule exists for when they do.

---

## 12. What stays hidden

The chapter does not answer:

- how many shards exist;
- what the Second Dawn was built to do beyond the rewind;
- who made it;
- whether the king knows what removing a shard does;
- what the Black Knight wants with the shards;
- whether the Guard will keep taking shards once they understand the cost.

The chapter only proves the pattern: **a shard changes the land around it, and taking it changes the
land again.**

---

## 13. The rule for later chapters

Greenwake is the first shard, so it establishes the pattern. It must not become the template for every
later shard.

Later chapters should vary **what the shard is feeding**:

- a person who needs it to live;
- a town that needs it to survive;
- a machine or a bargain that should not work;
- a creature that chose to keep it;
- a place that is better because of it.

The constant is not the bloom. The constant is the cost of removal.

## 14. Implementation boundaries

- No new art is required for this story lock. The bloom can be carried by the existing forest backdrop,
  the slime art, root props and scene text.
- Wave 1 is bats. The intro slime is a scene actor, not a wave. The stage wave plan is in place:
  `Stage01_WavePlan.asset` (6 normal waves: bats, first wolf, pack, last wolf at the wall, slimes; boss wave 7).
- The Slime King can remain the enlarged Slime_Art with a root crown; the story does not require a
  unique sprite.
- The mid and outro scenes must not add new systems. They are story beats around existing waves.
- The first-wipe scene must not reset the stage or duplicate loot. It restores the party and returns
  them to the last ground.
- Dialogue stays out of this page. `Story-Nodes.md` owns the words; this page owns the truth they must
  serve.
